Handmade vs mass-produced ceramics: what’s the difference?

Mass-produced ceramics are made in large runs, with processes designed so every piece comes out the same. Handmade ceramics are made one at a time: shaped by hand or on the wheel, left to dry, glazed and fired. Every mug and every bowl passes through the hands of the person who made it.

That’s why two “identical” pieces will show small differences in colour and shape. They aren’t flaws; they’re traces of the process. When we make a series, for a restaurant for example, we keep sizes and glaze consistent, but each piece is still one of a kind.

There’s a less visible difference too: when you buy from a small studio, you know who made your piece and you can ask them directly how to use it and look after it.

What to check before you buy

  • The clay. Stoneware is fired at high temperature and is a common choice for everyday tableware. All our pieces are high-fire stoneware.
  • How you’ll use it. If you’ll eat or drink from it, check it’s food safe and whether it can go in the dishwasher and microwave. Our custom-order pieces can, though they aren’t oven safe. In the online shop, read each product page.
  • Size. A mug can look bigger or smaller in a photo. Check the measurements before you decide.
  • Variation. If you buy several matching pieces, expect them to differ slightly.
  • Timing. Online shop orders are prepared within 24 hours. A personalised piece takes 2–3 weeks once the design is agreed.
