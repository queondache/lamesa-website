Pottery for Beginners in Barcelona: How to Get Started

By the La Mesa team · Ceramics studio in La Barceloneta

You can start pottery without an art background, either by shaping clay at a table or by trying the wheel. This guide from La Mesa, our studio in Barceloneta, introduces the basic techniques, the skills worth practising first and what happens between shaping your first piece and collecting it.

Should beginners start with hand-building or the wheel?

Hand-building lets you build and adjust a piece on the table. It suits an interest in texture, plates and asymmetric forms. The wheel is used for round forms and involves coordinating hand pressure with the movement of the clay.

You do not have to learn hand-building before taking a wheel class. Choose the process you are curious about. Our beginner's guide to wheel throwing explains what the first attempts involve.

Three hand-building techniques to know

Pinching: open a ball of clay and use your fingers to thin and shape the walls. A small bowl is a useful way to practise an even rim and wall thickness.

Coiling: build up a form with rolls of clay. Joining the coils properly turns separate layers into a continuous wall.

Slab building: use sheets of clay to create flat surfaces or assembled shapes. Thickness, firmness and careful joins matter here.

You will choose a technique to suit your project and the time available, rather than necessarily cover all three in one class. Keeping the first shape simple gives you room to notice how the clay responds.

What to practise before adding decoration

An even thickness, a stable base and sound joins help a piece hold its shape. Once those are in place, you can explore a handle, a textured surface or a more detailed rim.

Irregular shapes can be intentional; cracks need attention. Drying, weak joins and uneven thickness can all affect a piece. Your teacher can help you decide what to adjust before it goes through the kiln. A crack is not automatically a harmless sign of handmade work.

Do you need to buy tools?

At La Mesa, class materials and firings are included: clay, tools and glazes are provided. You do not need to buy a starter kit. Wear clothes you are comfortable getting dirty and bring your own apron if you prefer.

Discuss how you would like to use the piece. A decorative object and a bowl intended for food need appropriate finishing; not every first experiment will be suitable for everyday tableware.

How long until you have a finished piece?

You shape clay during the class, then leave it at the studio for drying, finishing and firing. You cannot take the raw piece home that day. The studio confirms the collection timeframe; mention any travel deadline before booking.

Progress varies from person to person. Repeating a simple form across several sessions gives you something concrete to compare: the thickness, the base and the way the rim feels. A weekly hand-building class gives you time to build on those observations.
