10 Things to Do in Barceloneta

By the La Mesa team · Ceramics studio in La Barceloneta

Barceloneta is a place to alternate the waterfront with neighbourhood streets: a walk, the food market, a museum or time making something with your hands. These ten ideas come from La Mesa, our pottery studio at Carrer de l'Atlàntida, 47. Choose a few and leave room to enjoy the way between them.

1. Walk along the Passeig Marítim

Follow the seafront between the area by Hospital del Mar and the W hotel. With the beach on one side and the neighbourhood on the other, you can walk a short stretch or make it the thread running through your day. Later in the afternoon, pause to watch the light change over the water.

2. Browse the Mercat de la Barceloneta

At Plaça del Poeta Boscà, 1–2, the market brings together everyday food shopping beneath a structure that retains iron elements from the original building. Browse the stalls and take a look at the building itself. The City Council's market listing provides practical visitor information.

3. Try pottery at La Mesa

At our studio, you can try a two-hour hand-building experience without previous experience. Shaping a bowl or plate at the table brings a different rhythm to a day of walking. Materials and firings are included; pieces stay for drying and firing and are collected later.

4. Take a break by the sea

A terrace near the beach can be a place to sit and talk between walks. Choose according to what you feel like ordering and the venue's menu. Or simply spend some time sitting near the water, without adding another booking to your day.

5. Explore the streets behind the waterfront

Sant Carles, la Maquinista and Balboa offer routes through the streets linking different parts of the neighbourhood. Notice the façades, balconies and small businesses: details that are easy to miss on a direct trip from the metro to the beach. Leave space for residents and respect their privacy.

6. Walk by Port Vell

On the harbour side, boats, quays and the waterfront buildings replace the sand. Include this stretch on your way to the museum and see another side of the neighbourhood's relationship with the water.

7. Stop for breakfast or an afternoon snack

A café on the streets inside the neighbourhood makes a pause before moving on. Rather than collecting a list of venues, choose one that fits your route and enjoy the time there. Before a pottery class, it can be a chance to talk about what you would like to make.

8. Visit the Museu d'Història de Catalunya

At Plaça de Pau Vila, 3, the museum presents exhibitions exploring Catalonia's history. It adds a cultural stop to the walk and gives you an indoor option. See the museum's official website for current exhibitions and visitor information.

9. Continue towards Sant Sebastià

Follow the seafront towards Sant Sebastià beach. Take this part of the route at your own pace, whether you want photographs of the view or simply time looking at the water. You do not need a full beach day to enjoy being near the sea.

10. Share tapas in the neighbourhood

Finish with a meal to share. Explore streets such as Ginebra and choose a venue according to the menu and what your group feels like eating. Sitting down together can be a way to end the walk or compare notes after your class.

Putting the day together

A morning could combine the market, a walk and a café; an afternoon, pottery and a meal. On a rainy day, consider the museum and studio. Check opening hours, menus and available spaces before arranging visits, and the forecast if the waterfront is central to the plan. Allow time between stops: the pottery experience takes the full two hours.
