Pottery Team Building in Barcelona: Planning Guide

By the La Mesa team · Ceramics studio in La Barceloneta

Pottery team building brings a group together to shape clay with guidance, without requiring previous experience. At La Mesa, sessions last two to three hours: up to ten people at our Barceloneta studio or up to forty at an external venue, subject to the space being suitable and the date being available.

This guide covers choosing a format, preparing participants and arranging collection of the pieces. Pottery offers a shared activity outside everyday work tasks; a single session does not guarantee improvements in team communication or performance.

What the activity adds to a team gathering

Working around a table gives people something concrete to do: build, observe, ask for help and share tools. Each participant can develop their own piece while sharing the space with colleagues.

Explain the activity in advance and avoid treating the finished work as an assessment. People arrive with different practical experience and different feelings about trying something unfamiliar. Allow time for questions, different working speeds and requests for support.

Hand-building or the wheel for a group?

Hand-building lets several people work at the table at the same time. Simple forms such as a bowl or plate can be adapted to the session length and group. The opening demonstration introduces the movements; developing control of the material takes practice.

Wheel throwing needs equipment and specific arrangements for teaching and turns. Our wheel experience has a maximum of two participants, so do not assume the same format automatically expands to a large team. Ask about hand-building for group events.

Planning the two or three hours

Allow time for arrival, a demonstration, making and the end of the session. If pottery is part of a day with meetings or a meal, discuss setup and clearing-away time before scheduling another activity immediately afterwards.

Before booking, share the number of people, possible dates and the language you need. We can teach in English, Spanish and Portuguese; confirm the language for your event. Also mention access requirements or support needed for working with the hands.

Ask for the proposal to specify materials, tools, firings and any additional services. Agree food, drinks or a particular group activity in advance. The confirmation should set out what is included and the conditions for changing or cancelling the event.

What happens to the pieces afterwards?

The pieces are not handed over on the day. Clay needs to dry and go through finishing and firing. Agree who will be the group's contact person and how the pieces will be identified for later collection.

If participants are travelling from another city, raise this when requesting the proposal. The timeframe and any delivery arrangements need to be confirmed with La Mesa before you promise a collection date to the group.

Mistakes to avoid

Turning it into a competition: if the purpose is to share an activity, there is no need to pick the best piece or compare people's skills.

Promising identical results: projects are adapted to the time and experience available. Handmade pieces will not be exact copies of each other.

Skipping practical information: tell participants they will be working with clay, recommend clothes that can get dirty and explain that their work will be collected later.

Comparing pottery with other team activities

Choose according to what the group wants from the gathering. Pottery adds guided manual practice; a meal centres on sharing a table and conversation; an outdoor activity needs consideration of weather, route and access. Compare actual proposals by duration, participation and your team's requirements, rather than assuming one activity works best for everyone.

To request a session, send participant numbers, date, location and language. For a celebration with friends, our pottery outing guide covers that type of gathering.

Frequently asked questions

What is pottery team building?

It is a group activity shaping clay with guidance. At La Mesa, it lasts two to three hours and pieces stay for drying and firing before collection.

Do we need previous experience?

No. The session can be adapted to beginners, with an initial demonstration and guidance. People practise at their own pace; a perfect piece is not guaranteed.

Is hand-building or the wheel better for a team?

Hand-building lets people work at the table at the same time. La Mesa’s wheel experience has a maximum of two participants; larger teams can request a hand-building proposal.

Can we take our pieces home on the day?

No. They need to dry and go through finishing and firing. La Mesa confirms the timeframe and arrangements for collection or any agreed delivery.

Can the activity take place at our office?

Yes, subject to the space being suitable. La Mesa assesses tables, water access, surfaces, setup and transport before confirming an off-site event.

How many people can take part?

La Mesa offers sessions for up to ten people at its Barceloneta studio and up to forty at an external venue, subject to availability and venue conditions.
