Wheel Throwing in Barcelona: A Beginner’s Guide

By the La Mesa team · Ceramics studio in La Barceloneta

Wheel throwing means shaping clay with your hands on a rotating pottery wheel. It is used to make round forms such as bowls and cylinders. Beginners can try it without previous pottery experience: a first class introduces centring, opening the clay and lifting the walls with guidance from a teacher.

Wheel throwing or hand-building?

Hand-building happens at a table, using pinching, coils or slabs. You can build symmetrical or irregular shapes and add parts gradually. On the wheel, rotation helps create the form and you coordinate pressure from both hands.

Neither is a prerequisite for the other. Hand-building may appeal if you want to explore texture or asymmetric shapes; the wheel may suit your curiosity about movement and circular forms. Our beginner's pottery guide introduces the hand-building techniques.

The first steps on the wheel

Centring: place and stabilise clay in the middle of the wheel. Off-centre clay wobbles and makes the walls harder to control. You learn by adjusting posture, support and pressure, with demonstrations and practice.

Opening: make the initial hollow and establish the base. Controlling the depth helps avoid pushing through the bottom.

Pulling the walls: move clay upwards with coordinated pressure inside and outside the form. A simple cylinder is useful practice before attempting a more complex profile.

Shaping and finishing the rim: adjust the outline without weakening the piece. Your teacher helps you recognise when to stop working the clay.

What to expect from your first attempts

A small bowl or cylinder is a possible starting project, but neither a set number of pieces nor a perfect shape is guaranteed. Part of learning is noticing when the clay loses stability and trying the movement again.

Not every practice piece will become everyday tableware. Thickness, drying, finishing and firing all affect the result. Tell the team whether you hope to make something decorative or a piece intended for the table.

The wheel experience at La Mesa

Our wheel class in Barceloneta lasts two hours, has a maximum of two participants and costs €65 per person. Clay, tools, glazes and firings are included. No previous experience is required; wear comfortable clothes you can get dirty.

The studio is at Carrer de l'Atlàntida, 47, Barcelona. Check the experience page for dates and booking options.

What happens after the session?

Your piece stays at the studio to dry and go through finishing and firing. It cannot go home with you that day; we confirm when it is ready for collection.

If you want to continue, weekly wheel classes give you time to practise centring and develop new forms. If working at a table appeals more, you can also try a hand-building experience.
